# SDR Certification Rubric

Internal enablement document. Used by Sales Trainers to certify SDRs at the end of core
training (Decision Gate 1), before an SDR is cleared to make live calls with real prospects.

The certification has two parts: a written product assessment and a simulated pitch. Each
SDR is scored across five dimensions, 1-10 per dimension. The trainer reviews the AI gap
analysis and makes the final pass/fail call. The AI recommendation is advisory, not binding.

Passing bar: an SDR should score 7 or above on every dimension. A score of 5-6 on any one
dimension is a borderline result and requires trainer review. A score of 4 or below on any
dimension is a fail on that dimension regardless of the others, because each dimension maps
to a skill the SDR will need on their first live call.

---

## Dimension 1 — Product knowledge accuracy

What it measures: whether the SDR correctly describes Salesforce products and positions them
truthfully. SDRs who arrive at live calls with surface-level or incorrect product knowledge
damage credibility and the Salesforce brand.

What a passing answer (7-10) looks like:
- Correctly names and distinguishes the core clouds: Sales Cloud (pipeline and opportunity
  management), Service Cloud (customer service and case management), Marketing Cloud, Data
  Cloud, and the Agentforce AI layer.
- States what a product does in plain business terms, not feature lists.
- Does not overstate capabilities or invent features.

What a weak answer (1-4) looks like:
- Confuses one cloud for another (e.g. describes Service Cloud functions as Sales Cloud).
- Lists features without explaining the business outcome.
- Makes claims that are not accurate.

Common gap: SDRs know their primary product area but are vague on products outside their
assigned vertical. Coaching action: targeted product modules for the weak cloud.

---

## Dimension 2 — Objection handling

What it measures: whether the SDR handles prospect pushback using a structured method rather
than reacting defensively. Objections commonly surface as early as the cold call.

The expected method is the three-step Defuse, Discover, Deliver approach:
- Defuse: acknowledge the objection and the emotion behind it. An objection is better than a
  flat no, so the first move is to thank the prospect and acknowledge the concern, which
  builds trust and keeps the conversation open.
- Discover: ask open-ended questions to find the real concern behind the surface objection.
  The first objection is usually superficial; the actual concern sits underneath it.
- Deliver: respond to the real concern, reframing it as a message of value.

What a passing answer (7-10) looks like:
- Acknowledges the objection before responding to it.
- Asks a clarifying question rather than immediately firing back a counterpoint.
- Reframes the concern into a value conversation.
- Example of strong handling on a price objection: acknowledge, ask what is driving the
  budget concern, then connect the product to a quantified business outcome rather than
  defending the price.

What a weak answer (1-4) looks like:
- Becomes defensive or argues with the prospect.
- Responds to the surface objection without uncovering the real concern.
- Reads as a scripted rebuttal rather than a conversation.

Common gap: new SDRs react defensively to valid objections. Coaching action: objection
roleplay focused on the Discover step.

---

## Dimension 3 — MEDDIC framework application

What it measures: whether the SDR can qualify an opportunity using MEDDIC, the B2B
qualification framework that determines whether a deal is real and worth an AE's time.

MEDDIC stands for:
- Metrics: the quantifiable business outcome the solution would deliver. Metrics create
  urgency and justify the investment with a clear return.
- Economic Buyer: the individual with the authority to give final approval and release
  budget. Not just any contact, the person who can say yes.
- Decision Criteria: the formal requirements the prospect will use to evaluate solutions.
- Decision Process: the steps, approvals, and timeline the prospect will follow to buy.
- Identify Pain: the core business problem driving the prospect to act, with the impact of
  that problem quantified where possible.
- Champion: an internal advocate with the influence and credibility to sell on the rep's
  behalf inside the prospect's organization.

What a passing answer (7-10) looks like:
- Correctly identifies which MEDDIC elements are present and which are missing in a given
  scenario.
- Knows that identifying the economic buyer is distinct from talking to any stakeholder.
- Understands that pain should be quantified, not just named.
- Recognizes that a deal missing the economic buyer or a champion is not yet qualified.

What a weak answer (1-4) looks like:
- Treats any friendly contact as the economic buyer.
- Names a pain point without any business impact attached.
- Cannot distinguish a qualified opportunity from an unqualified one.

Common gap: SDRs conflate a helpful contact with the economic buyer, and book meetings that
the AE later rejects. Coaching action: MEDDIC qualification drills. Note: poor qualification
is the single most common reason SDR-booked meetings fail to convert, so this dimension is
weighted heavily in the pass/fail decision.

## Dimension 4 — Salesforce value messaging

What it measures: whether the SDR sells the business outcome rather than the feature. The
goal is value-based positioning: proving the solution delivers a specific, quantifiable
return, which shifts the conversation away from price and toward investment.

What a passing answer (7-10) looks like:
- Leads with the prospect's business problem and the outcome the product enables.
- Quantifies value where possible (time saved, pipeline gained, cost avoided).
- Frames the product as an investment with a return, not a cost.

What a weak answer (1-4) looks like:
- Lists product features with no connection to a business outcome.
- Cannot articulate why the prospect should care.
- Defends on price instead of reframing to value.

Common gap: feature-dumping instead of outcome-selling. Coaching action: value messaging
practice tied to specific personas.

---

## Dimension 5 — Discovery and questioning

What it measures: whether the SDR asks effective questions to understand the prospect before
pitching. Strong discovery is what separates a qualifying conversation from a pitch at a
stranger.

What a passing answer (7-10) looks like:
- Asks open-ended questions that surface pain, priorities, and context.
- Listens and builds on the prospect's answers rather than running a fixed script.
- Uses discovery to gather the information needed to qualify against MEDDIC.
- Confirms understanding by reflecting the prospect's concern back to them.

What a weak answer (1-4) looks like:
- Jumps straight to a pitch without understanding the prospect.
- Asks only closed yes/no questions.
- Reads a script verbatim and sounds robotic.

Common gap: SDRs pitch before they understand. Coaching action: discovery question bank
practice and active-listening drills.

---

## How to read the overall result

- All five dimensions at 7 or above: recommend PASS.
- Any dimension at 5-6, rest passing: recommend BORDERLINE, send to trainer review. The
  trainer weighs trajectory and context (a borderline score may reflect a bad day rather
  than a real gap).
- Any dimension at 4 or below: recommend FAIL on that dimension, with the specific gap and
  the coaching action named.

The gap analysis should state, per dimension, the score, the specific evidence from the
SDR's answers that justifies it, and the gap to close. The trainer makes the final
certification decision and records a required comment.
