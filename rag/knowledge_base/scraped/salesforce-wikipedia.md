**Salesforce, Inc.**, is an American [cloud](/wiki/Cloud_computing "Cloud computing")-based software company headquartered in [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), California. It is primarily known for its [customer relationship management](/wiki/Customer_relationship_management "Customer relationship management") software and related applications which it delivers through a [software as a service](/wiki/Software_as_a_service "Software as a service") subscription business model.[[2]](#cite_note-2) It also provides software for [sales](/wiki/Sales "Sales"), [customer service](/wiki/Customer_service "Customer service"), [marketing automation](/wiki/Marketing_automation "Marketing automation"), [e-commerce](/wiki/E-commerce "E-commerce"), [analytics](/wiki/Analytics "Analytics"), [artificial intelligence](/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"), [agentic AI](/wiki/Agentic_AI "Agentic AI"), and application development.

Founded by former [Oracle](/wiki/Oracle_Corporation "Oracle Corporation") executive [Marc Benioff](/wiki/Marc_Benioff "Marc Benioff") in March 1999, Salesforce grew quickly, making its [initial public offering](/wiki/Initial_public_offering "Initial public offering") in 2004. Its major acquisitions included [Tableau](/wiki/Tableau_Software "Tableau Software") and [Slack](/wiki/Slack_(software) "Slack (software)"). As of March 2026, Salesforce has a [market capitalization](/wiki/Market_capitalization "Market capitalization") of approximately US$186 billion.[[3]](#cite_note-3) For fiscal year 2026 (ending January 31, 2026), the company reported record annual revenue of $41.5 billion.[[4]](#cite_note-4) The company is a component of the [Dow Jones Industrial Average](/wiki/Dow_Jones_Industrial_Average "Dow Jones Industrial Average") (since 2020) and the [S&P 500](/wiki/S%26P_500 "S&P 500").

## History

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

Salesforce was founded on March 8, 1999, by former [Oracle](/wiki/Oracle_Corporation "Oracle Corporation") executive [Marc Benioff](/wiki/Marc_Benioff "Marc Benioff"), together with Parker Harris, Dave Moellenhoff, and Frank Dominguez as a [software-as-a-service](/wiki/Software_as_a_service "Software as a service") (SaaS) company.[[5]](#cite_note-5)[[6]](#cite_note-:0-6)[[7]](#cite_note-:7-7) The first prototype of Salesforce was launched in November 1999.[[7]](#cite_note-:7-7)

Two of Salesforce's earliest investors were [Larry Ellison](/wiki/Larry_Ellison "Larry Ellison"), the co-founder and first CEO of [Oracle](/wiki/Oracle_Corporation "Oracle Corporation"), and [Halsey Minor](/wiki/Halsey_Minor "Halsey Minor"), the founder of [CNET](/wiki/CNET "CNET").[[6]](#cite_note-:0-6)

Salesforce was severely affected by the [dot-com bubble](/wiki/Dot-com_bubble "Dot-com bubble") bursting at the beginning of the new millennium, resulting in the company laying off 20% of its workforce. Despite its losses, Salesforce continued strong during the early 2000s. Salesforce also gained notability during this period for its "the end of software" tagline and marketing campaign, and even hired actors to hold up signs with its slogan outside a [Siebel Systems](/wiki/Siebel_Systems "Siebel Systems") conference.[[8]](#cite_note-8) Salesforce's revenue continued to increase, moving from $5.4 million in 2000 to $22.4 million in 2001.[[9]](#cite_note-:4-9)[[10]](#cite_note-10)

In 2003, Salesforce held its first annual Dreamforce conference in San Francisco.[[11]](#cite_note-11) There were around 1,300 attendees and 50 exhibitors at the conference.[[12]](#cite_note-12)

In June 2004, the company had its [initial public offering](/wiki/Initial_public_offering "Initial public offering") on the [New York Stock Exchange](/wiki/New_York_Stock_Exchange "New York Stock Exchange") (under the stock symbol CRM) and raised US$110 million.[[13]](#cite_note-Compton_2004-06-23-13)[[14]](#cite_note-14) In 2006, Salesforce launched Idea Exchange, a platform that allows customers to connect with company product managers.[[15]](#cite_note-15)

In 2009, Salesforce passed $1 billion in annual revenue.[[6]](#cite_note-:0-6) Also, in 2009, the company launched Service Cloud, an application that helps companies manage service conversations about their products and services.[[16]](#cite_note-16)

In 2014, the company released Trailhead, a free online learning platform.[[17]](#cite_note-:3-17) In October 2014, Salesforce announced the development of its Customer Success Platform.[[18]](#cite_note-18) In September 2016, Salesforce announced the launch of Einstein, an artificial intelligence platform that supports several of Salesforce's cloud services.[[19]](#cite_note-19)[[20]](#cite_note-20) Salesforce licensed the right to use [Albert Einstein](/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein")'s image and likeness from the Einstein Estate under a 20-year agreement for $20 million, restricted to business software applications.[[21]](#cite_note-21)

Salesforce acquired the visual analytics and business intelligence software platform [Tableau](/wiki/Tableau_Software "Tableau Software") in August 2019 for $15.7 billion.

In 2020, Salesforce joined the [Dow Jones Industrial Average](/wiki/Dow_Jones_Industrial_Average "Dow Jones Industrial Average"), replacing energy giant and [Standard Oil](/wiki/Standard_Oil "Standard Oil")-descendant [ExxonMobil](/wiki/ExxonMobil "ExxonMobil").[[22]](#cite_note-Stevens-22) Salesforce's ascension to the Dow Jones was concurrent with that of [Amgen](/wiki/Amgen "Amgen") and [Honeywell](/wiki/Honeywell "Honeywell").[[23]](#cite_note-:5-23) Because the Dow Jones factors its components by market price, Salesforce was the largest technology component of the index at its accession.[[24]](#cite_note-24)

Salesforce experienced significant leadership transitions between 2020 and 2021; Keith Block stepped down as co-CEO in February 2020,[[25]](#cite_note-TechCrunch-25) leaving Marc Benioff as sole chairman and CEO.[[26]](#cite_note-26) In February 2021, Weaver, previously the chief legal officer, became CFO, where she served until stepping down in August 2024.[[27]](#cite_note-27) Former CFO Mark Hawkins announced that he would be retiring in October.[[28]](#cite_note-Trentmann-28)[[29]](#cite_note-Ovide-29) In November 2021, [Bret Taylor](/wiki/Bret_Taylor "Bret Taylor") was named vice chair and co-CEO of the company alongside Benioff.[[30]](#cite_note-30)

In December 2020, Salesforce announced its acquisition of [Slack](/wiki/Slack_Technologies "Slack Technologies") for $27.7 billion, the largest in company history;[[31]](#cite_note-31) the deal closed in July 2021.[[32]](#cite_note-32) The purchase price represented a 54% premium over Slack's closing market capitalization at the announcement date, valued at approximately $18 billion. Salesforce justified the premium based on anticipated synergies from integrating Slack's messaging platform with its CRM services.[[33]](#cite_note-33)[*[better source needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]

Salesforce launched the Sustainability Cloud (Net Zero Cloud as of 2022[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit)), which is used by companies to track progress towards achieving their [net zero emissions](/wiki/Net-zero_emissions "Net-zero emissions") goals.[[34]](#cite_note-34)

In April 2022, "Salesforce.com, Inc." changed its legal name to "Salesforce, Inc."[[35]](#cite_note-35)

In August 2022, Salesforce surpassed [SAP](/wiki/SAP "SAP") in total revenue and market capitalization to become the world's largest enterprise applications software company, according to industry analysis.[[36]](#cite_note-:6-36)

The next month, Salesforce announced a partnership with [Meta Platforms](/wiki/Meta_Platforms "Meta Platforms"). The deal called for Meta's consumer application [WhatsApp](/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp") to integrate Salesforce's Customer 360 platform to allow consumers to communicate with companies directly.[[37]](#cite_note-37)

In November 2022, Salesforce announced it would terminate some employees from its sales team.[[38]](#cite_note-38) That same month, Salesforce announced its co-CEO and vice chair, Bret Taylor, would be stepping down from his roles at the end of January 2023, with Benioff continuing to run the company and serve as board chair.[[39]](#cite_note-39) Within the week, former [Tableau](/wiki/Tableau_Software "Tableau Software") CEO Mark Nelson and former [Slack](/wiki/Slack_(software) "Slack (software)") CEO [Stewart Butterfield](/wiki/Stewart_Butterfield "Stewart Butterfield") also announced their departures. When asked about departures, Benioff stated, "people come and people go." Following these executive transitions, Salesforce's stock declined significantly, with market analysts attributing the decline to concerns about leadership stability and strategic continuity.[[40]](#cite_note-40)[[41]](#cite_note-41)[[42]](#cite_note-42)[[43]](#cite_note-43)

In January 2023, the company announced a [layoff](/wiki/Layoff "Layoff") of about 10%, or approximately 8,000 positions.[[44]](#cite_note-:13-44) According to Benioff, the company hired too aggressively during the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") and the increase in working from home led to the layoff. The company also reduced office space as part of the restructuring plan.[[44]](#cite_note-:13-44) The same month brought an announcement from [activist investor](/wiki/Activist_investor "Activist investor") [Elliott Management](/wiki/Elliott_Investment_Management "Elliott Investment Management") that it would acquire a "big stake" in the company.[[45]](#cite_note-45)

In January 2024, Salesforce announced it was laying off 700 of its global staff.[[46]](#cite_note-46)

In October 2025, Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff declared his avid support of [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") and urged him to deploy the [National Guard](/wiki/National_Guard_(United_States) "National Guard (United States)") to San Francisco as Trump was [deploying federal troops to other cities](/wiki/2025_deployment_of_federal_forces_in_the_United_States "2025 deployment of federal forces in the United States").[[47]](#cite_note-47) The comments generated significant controversy for Benioff and Salesforce,[[48]](#cite_note-48) including push-back from San Francisco mayor [Daniel Lurie](/wiki/Daniel_Lurie "Daniel Lurie") and District Attorney [Brooke Jenkins](/wiki/Brooke_Jenkins "Brooke Jenkins")[[49]](#cite_note-49) and the resignation of [Ron Conway](/wiki/Ron_Conway "Ron Conway") from the Salesforce Foundation board.[[50]](#cite_note-50) Benioff subsequently apologized for his National Guard comments.[[51]](#cite_note-51) That same month, Salesforce pitched the [Trump administration](/wiki/Second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump "Second presidency of Donald Trump") on ways that Salesforce's AI tools could help [Immigration and Customs Enforcement](/wiki/United_States_Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement "United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement") (ICE) triple its staff to support the administration's [mass deportation campaign](/wiki/Deportation_in_the_second_Trump_administration "Deportation in the second Trump administration").[[52]](#cite_note-:8-52) The company had previously contracted for ICE under the Obama and Biden administrations.[[52]](#cite_note-:8-52)

## Services

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Services")]

Salesforce offers several [customer relationship management](/wiki/Customer_relationship_management "Customer relationship management") (CRM) services for enterprises and small businesses, including Sales Cloud,[[53]](#cite_note-53) Service Cloud,[[54]](#cite_note-54) [Marketing Cloud](/wiki/Salesforce_Marketing_Cloud "Salesforce Marketing Cloud"),[[55]](#cite_note-:1-55)Commerce Cloud and Platform.[[55]](#cite_note-:1-55) Additional technologies include Slack.[[56]](#cite_note-56)Salesforce also offers a free CRM for small teams.[[57]](#cite_note-57)

Other services include app creation, [data integration](/wiki/Data_integration "Data integration") and visualization, and training.[[58]](#cite_note-58)

Salesforce launched a suite of features called Salesforce Foundations in September 2024, bundling connected functionality across department-specific Sales Cloud and Service Cloud products.[[59]](#cite_note-59)

### Artificial intelligence

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Artificial intelligence")]

Launched at Dreamforce in 2016, Salesforce Einstein was the company's first artificial intelligence product.[[60]](#cite_note-60)

In March 2023, Salesforce announced [ChatGPT](/wiki/ChatGPT "ChatGPT") integration in Slack was available to any organization, and the launch of Einstein GPT, a generative AI service.[[61]](#cite_note-61)

In March 2024, Salesforce launched Einstein Copilot: Health Actions, an assistant based on its earlier artificial intelligence platform Einstein.[[62]](#cite_note-62) It can be used to assist in making appointments, referrals, and gathering patient information.[[63]](#cite_note-63) In July, Salesforce released an [AI agent](/wiki/AI_agent "AI agent") with the ability to perform customer service actions, like enabling product returns or refunds.[[64]](#cite_note-64)[[65]](#cite_note-65)

In September and October 2024, the company deployed Agentforce (succeeding Salesforce Einstein), an [agentic AI](/wiki/Agentic_AI "Agentic AI").[[66]](#cite_note-66)[[67]](#cite_note-67) This enables the use of chatbots inside its software.[[68]](#cite_note-68)

Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff said in a June 2025 interview that artificial intelligence performed between 30% and 50% of internal work at Salesforce, including functions such as software engineering, customer service, marketing, and analytics.[[69]](#cite_note-FastCo-69)[[70]](#cite_note-FortuneAI-70)

### Salesforce Platform

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Salesforce Platform")]

Salesforce Platform (formerly known as Force.com) is a [platform as a service](/wiki/Platform_as_a_service "Platform as a service") (PaaS) that allows developers to add applications to the main Salesforce.com application.[[71]](#cite_note-71)[*[failed verification](/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*] These applications are hosted on Salesforce.com infrastructure.[[72]](#cite_note-72)

Force.com applications are built using Apex, a proprietary [Java](/wiki/Java_(programming_language) "Java (programming language)")-like programming language,[[73]](#cite_note-73) to generate [HTML](/wiki/HTML "HTML") originally via the "Visualforce" framework. Beginning in 2015,[[74]](#cite_note-74) the "Lightning Components" framework has been supported. The Apex language and compiler were initially designed by Craig Weissman.[[75]](#cite_note-75)

As of 2014[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit), the Force.com platform had 1.5 million registered developers according to Salesforce.[[9]](#cite_note-:4-9)

### AppExchange

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: AppExchange")]

Launched in 2005, the Salesforce AppExchange is an online [app store](/wiki/App_store "App store") that allows users to sell third-party applications and consulting services.[[76]](#cite_note-76)[[77]](#cite_note-77)

### Trailhead

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Trailhead")]

Launched in 2014, Trailhead is a free online learning platform with courses focused on Salesforce technologies.[[78]](#cite_note-78)[[79]](#cite_note-79)[[17]](#cite_note-:3-17)

### Discontinued

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Discontinued")]

Desk.com was a [SaaS](/wiki/Software_as_a_service "Software as a service") [help desk](/wiki/Help_desk "Help desk") and [customer support](/wiki/Customer_support "Customer support") product acquired by Salesforce for $50 million in 2011,[[80]](#cite_note-Techcrunch-80)[[81]](#cite_note-PC_World-81) and consolidated with other services into Service Cloud Essentials in March 2018.[[82]](#cite_note-82)

## Acquisitions

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Acquisitions")]

### 2006–2015

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: 2006–2015")]

In 2006, Salesforce acquired Sendia, a mobile web service firm, for $15 million[[86]](#cite_note-86) and Kieden, an online advertising company.[[87]](#cite_note-87) In 2007, Koral, a content management service, was acquired.[[88]](#cite_note-88) In 2008, Salesforce acquired Instranet for $31.5 million.[[89]](#cite_note-89) In 2010, Salesforce acquired multiple companies, including Jigsaw, a cloud-based data service provider, for $142 million,[[90]](#cite_note-90) [Heroku](/wiki/Heroku "Heroku"), a Ruby application platform-as-a-service, for $212 million,[[91]](#cite_note-foobar-91) and Activa Live Chat, a live chat software provider.[[92]](#cite_note-92)
In 2011, Salesforce acquired [Dimdim](/wiki/Dimdim "Dimdim"), a web conferencing platform, for $31 million,[[93]](#cite_note-93) Radian6, a social media tracking company, for $340 million,[[94]](#cite_note-manymoon-94) and Rypple, a performance management software company.[[95]](#cite_note-95) Rypple became known as Work.com in 2012.[[96]](#cite_note-96) In 2012, Salesforce acquired Buddy Media, a social media marketer, for $689 million,[[97]](#cite_note-97) and GoInstant, a browser collaboration startup, for $70 million.[[98]](#cite_note-98)

In 2013, Salesforce acquired ExactTarget, an email marketer, for $2.5 billion.[[99]](#cite_note-99) In 2014, Salesforce acquired [RelateIQ](/wiki/RelateIQ "RelateIQ"), a data company, for $390 million.[[100]](#cite_note-100) In 2015, Salesforce acquired multiple companies for undisclosed sums, including Toopher, a mobile authentication company,[[101]](#cite_note-101) [Tempo](/wiki/Tempo_(app) "Tempo (app)"), an AI calendar app,[[102]](#cite_note-102) and MinHash, an AI platform.[[103]](#cite_note-103) The company also acquired SteelBrick, a software company, for $360 million.[[104]](#cite_note-104)

### 2016–present

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: 2016–present")]

In 2016, Salesforce acquired [Demandware](/wiki/Demandware "Demandware"), a cloud-based provider of e-commerce services, for $2.8 billion[[105]](#cite_note-105)[[106]](#cite_note-106) and [Quip](/wiki/Quip_(software) "Quip (software)"), a word processing app, for $750 million.[[107]](#cite_note-107) In 2017, the company acquired Sequence, a user experience design agency.[[108]](#cite_note-108) In 2018, Salesforce acquired several companies, including [MuleSoft](/wiki/MuleSoft "MuleSoft"), a cloud service company, for $6.5 billion,[[109]](#cite_note-109)[[110]](#cite_note-110) as well as Rebel, an email services provider,[[111]](#cite_note-111) and Datorama, an AI marketing platform.[[112]](#cite_note-112)

In 2019, Salesforce completed its acquisition of analytics software company [Tableau](/wiki/Tableau_Software "Tableau Software") for $15.7 billion[[113]](#cite_note-113). [Slack Technologies](/wiki/Slack_Technologies "Slack Technologies") was acquired for $27.7 billion in 2021.[[114]](#cite_note-114) Salesforce also made smaller acquisitions between 2019 and 2021, including [ClickSoftware](/wiki/ClickSoftware_Technologies "ClickSoftware Technologies") for $1.35 billion,[[115]](#cite_note-115) consulting firm Acumen Solutions for $570 million,[[116]](#cite_note-116) CRM firm Vlocity for $1.33 billion,[[25]](#cite_note-TechCrunch-25) privacy compliance startup Phennecs for $16.5 million,[[117]](#cite_note-117) and robotic process automation firm Servicetrace.[[118]](#cite_note-118)

Salesforce's acquired Slack-bot maker Troops.ai in July 2022.[[119]](#cite_note-119)[[120]](#cite_note-120)

In September 2023, Salesforce acquired Airkit.ai, a creator of AI-powered customer service applications and experiences.[[121]](#cite_note-121) In December 2023, Salesforce acquired Spiff, an automated commission management platform.[[122]](#cite_note-122)

In September 2024, Salesforce acquired data management firm Own for $1.9 billion,[[123]](#cite_note-123) and enterprise management platform Zoomin.[[124]](#cite_note-124) Salesforce has also acquired PredictSpring and Tenyx in 2024.[[125]](#cite_note-125)

In June 2025, Salesforce announced plans to acquire data management platform [Informatica](/wiki/Informatica "Informatica") for approximately $8 billion. The deal strengthens Salesforce's data integration and governance capabilities across its platform ecosystem. Prior negotiations in 2024 failed due to disagreements over valuation and deal structure.[[126]](#cite_note-126)

On November 18, 2025, Salesforce announced the successful completion of its acquisition of [Informatica](/wiki/Informatica "Informatica") for approximately $8 billion in equity value.[[127]](#cite_note-127) The deal was completed ahead of the original early-2026 target. By February 2026, Salesforce reported that Informatica had already begun contributing significantly to its financial results, including nearly $400 million in revenue during the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2026.[[128]](#cite_note-128)

### Culture

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Culture")]

According to Marc Benioff, Salesforce corporate culture is based on '[Ohana](/wiki/Ohana "Ohana"),' a Hawaiian concept emphasizing family and community. Benioff has described this philosophy as prioritizing employee well-being and broader stakeholder relationships.[[133]](#cite_note-133)

In 2021, Cynthia Perry, a design research senior manager, publicly resigned from Salesforce, posting her resignation letter on LinkedIn and alleging [workplace discrimination](/wiki/Workplace_discrimination "Workplace discrimination") related to diversity and inclusion practices, and posting her resignation letter on [LinkedIn](/wiki/LinkedIn "LinkedIn").[[134]](#cite_note-134)

On September 10, 2021, Benioff tweeted that the company is prepared to help any employee who wishes to move out of the state of [Texas](/wiki/Texas "Texas"), following [abortion legislation in Texas](/wiki/Abortion_in_Texas "Abortion in Texas"), announced on September 1, 2021.[[135]](#cite_note-135)

### IT infrastructure

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: IT infrastructure")]

In 2008, Salesforce migrated from [Sun Fire E25K](/wiki/Sun_Fire_15K#Sun_Fire_E25K "Sun Fire 15K") servers with [SPARC](/wiki/SPARC "SPARC") processors running [Solaris](/wiki/Oracle_Solaris "Oracle Solaris"), to [Dell](/wiki/Dell "Dell") servers with [AMD](/wiki/AMD "AMD") processors, running Linux.[[138]](#cite_note-138)

In 2013, Salesforce and Oracle announced a nine-year partnership focusing on applications, platforms, and infrastructure.[[141]](#cite_note-141)

In 2016, Salesforce announced that it will use [Amazon Web Services](/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services "Amazon Web Services") hosting for countries with restrictive data residency requirements and where no Salesforce data centers are operating.[[142]](#cite_note-142)

## Cybersecurity incidents

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Cybersecurity incidents")]

### 2007 Phishing attack

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: 2007 Phishing attack")]

In November 2007, a [phishing](/wiki/Phishing "Phishing") attack compromised contact information on a number of Salesforce customers. Some customers then received phishing emails that appeared to be invoices from Salesforce.[[143]](#cite_note-Berlind-143)[[144]](#cite_note-144) A Salesforce employee's credentials were stolen through social engineering. Salesforce stated the incident resulted from human manipulation rather than a software vulnerability in its platform.[[143]](#cite_note-Berlind-143)

### 2022 Heroku OAuth token theft (Salesforce subsidiary)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: 2022 Heroku OAuth token theft (Salesforce subsidiary)")]

On April 13, 2022, [GitHub](/wiki/GitHub "GitHub") notified [Heroku](/wiki/Heroku "Heroku") of unauthorized access to its systems through a compromised OAuth token. Heroku immediately revoked all affected tokens and notified customers to update their credentials.[[145]](#cite_note-145)[[146]](#cite_note-146)[[147]](#cite_note-147)

### 2025-2026 ShinyHunters targeted campaigns against Salesforce

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: 2025-2026 ShinyHunters targeted campaigns against Salesforce")]

[ShinyHunters](/wiki/ShinyHunters "ShinyHunters") has conducted several widespread data theft and extortion campaigns against Salesforce and thousands of its customers as noted by Google's Threat Intelligence Group/[Mandiant](/wiki/Mandiant "Mandiant"), Salesforce, and the [FBI](/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation").[[148]](#cite_note-:9-148)[[149]](#cite_note-:10-149)[[150]](#cite_note-:11-150)[[151]](#cite_note-:12-151)

* UNC6040 Modified Data Loader / [Vishing](/wiki/Vishing "Vishing") - ShinyHunters [threat actors](/wiki/Threat_actor "Threat actor") executed a [social engineering (security)](/wiki/Social_engineering_(security) "Social engineering (security)") campaign in which employees were persuaded to install a tampered version of Salesforce Data Loader. The [Trojan horse (computing)](/wiki/Trojan_horse_(computing) "Trojan horse (computing)") client functioned as a [malware](/wiki/Malware "Malware") dropper, enabling credential theft, unauthorized access, and large-scale data exfiltration, which was subsequently used for extortion against multiple organizations. Salesforce confirmed the campaign abused user trust and [vishing](/wiki/Vishing "Vishing") techniques, clarifying that no vulnerability in the Salesforce platform itself was exploited.[[152]](#cite_note-152)[[153]](#cite_note-153)[[154]](#cite_note-154)[[155]](#cite_note-155)

* ShinyHunters/UNC6395[[156]](#cite_note-156) [threat actors](/wiki/Threat_actor "Threat actor") compromised [OAuth](/wiki/OAuth "OAuth") and refresh tokens through the [Salesloft](/wiki/Salesloft "Salesloft")-Drift integration, enabling unauthorized access to Salesforce customer instance.[[157]](#cite_note-157) In response, Salesforce and [Salesloft](/wiki/Salesloft "Salesloft") revoked all affected Drift tokens and removed the [application software](/wiki/Application_software "Application software") from AppExchange pending investigation.[[158]](#cite_note-158)[[159]](#cite_note-159)[[160]](#cite_note-160)
* ShinyHunters/UNC6395 threat actors compromised another OAuth and refresh tokens through Gainsight integration,[[161]](#cite_note-161) enabling unauthorized access to Salesforce customer instances. In response, Salesforce and Gainsight revoked all their integration apps pending further investigation.[[162]](#cite_note-162)
* Salesforce released another major security advisory linking a "known threat group", believed to be ShinyHunters, to exploiting misconfigurations in their Salesforce Experience Cloud software.[[163]](#cite_note-163) The hacking group claimed responsibility for these data theft attacks and issued a warning to affected Salesforce customers to pay up or their data would be leaked. The hacking group claimed to have breached about 400 companies.[[164]](#cite_note-164)[[165]](#cite_note-165)

## Controversies

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Controversies")]

### ‘Meatpistol’ presenters fired at Def Con

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: ‘Meatpistol’ presenters fired at Def Con")]

In 2017, two Salesforce security engineers were fired after giving a presentation at [DEF CON](/wiki/DEF_CON "DEF CON") discussing a Salesforce [pentesting](/wiki/Penetration_test "Penetration test") framework called MEATPISTOL.[[166]](#cite_note-:2-166) MEATPISTOL was a Salesforce-developed exploit framework similar to (and named after) [Metasploit](/wiki/Metasploit "Metasploit"). Salesforce had previously planned to [open source](/wiki/Open_source "Open source") the framework, but changed plans just before the presentation, and fired them immediately afterward[[166]](#cite_note-:2-166) The presenters were sent a message half an hour before the presentation telling them to cancel the talk, but they did not see the message until afterward.[[166]](#cite_note-:2-166)[[167]](#cite_note-167) The terminated employees called on the company to [open-source](/wiki/Open-source "Open-source") the [software](/wiki/Software "Software") after being dismissed.[[168]](#cite_note-168)

### RAICES donation refusal

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: RAICES donation refusal")]

In 2018, the [not-for-profit](/wiki/Nonprofit_organization "Nonprofit organization") organization [Refugee and Immigrant Center for Education and Legal Services](/wiki/Refugee_and_Immigrant_Center_for_Education_and_Legal_Services "Refugee and Immigrant Center for Education and Legal Services") (RAICES) rejected a US$250,000 donation from Salesforce, citing the company's contracts to provide technology services to [U.S. Customs and Border Protection](/wiki/U.S._Customs_and_Border_Protection "U.S. Customs and Border Protection"). RAICES stated that accepting funds from a company supporting immigration enforcement conflicted with its mission to advocate for immigrant and refugee rights.[[169]](#cite_note-169)

### 2018 taxes

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: 2018 taxes")]

According to an [Institute on Taxation and Economic Policy](/wiki/Institute_on_Taxation_and_Economic_Policy "Institute on Taxation and Economic Policy") analysis published in December 2019, Salesforce paid no federal income tax on its 2018 profits, achieving a 0% effective tax rate. The report attributed this to provisions in the 2017 [Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017](/wiki/Tax_Cuts_and_Jobs_Act_of_2017 "Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017") and available tax credits and deductions. Their findings were published in a report based on the 379 [*Fortune* 500](/wiki/Fortune_500 "Fortune 500") companies that declared a profit in 2018.[[170]](#cite_note-170)

### Sex-trafficking lawsuit

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Sex-trafficking lawsuit")]

In March 2019, Salesforce faced a lawsuit by 50 anonymous women claiming to be victims and survivors of [sex trafficking](/wiki/Sex_trafficking_in_the_United_States "Sex trafficking in the United States"), abuse, and rape, alleging the company profited from and helped build technology that facilitated sex trafficking on the now-defunct [Backpage](/wiki/Backpage "Backpage").com.[[171]](#cite_note-FortuneBackpage-171) In March 2021, a judge granted partial dismissal of the case, dismissing charges of negligence and conspiracy, but allowed the case to proceed regarding charges of sex trafficking.[[172]](#cite_note-172) In March 2024, the case was dismissed without prejudice, meaning the plaintiffs could potentially refile their claims in the future.[[173]](#cite_note-173) In September 2024, the [US Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit](/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Ninth_Circuit "United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit") denied a motion to reverse the dismissal.[[174]](#cite_note-174)

### Disability discrimination lawsuit in Japan

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Disability discrimination lawsuit in Japan")]

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
|  | This section needs to be **updated**. Please help update this article to reflect recent events or newly available information. *(May 2026)* |

IIn July 2021, a former Salesforce Japan employee sued the company in Tokyo District Court, alleging discrimination based on disabilities (autism and ADHD), including denial of reasonable workplace accommodations. At that time, Japanese law required private companies to employ at least 2.3% disabled employees or pay a mandatory levy. Salesforce Japan reportedly failed to meet this threshold from 2009 to 2021 (except in 2017) and did not report the number of disabled employees to labor officials in 2020.[174] The firm declined to comment on the suit to the media. The ex-employee, who is autistic with ADHD, claimed she was discriminated against because of her disability and was terminated from the firm's Japanese web marketing team. The suit alleged that the anonymous woman, as an employee at Salesforce Japan from 2018 to 2020, faced hate speech, microaggressions, and rejection of reasonable accommodation from the manager. She alleged that her attempts to resolve the problem were met with pressure from HR and a job coach. As of 2026, no public record of a resolution to this case has been reported.[[175]](#cite_note-175)

### Employee layoffs/Matthew McConaughey's salary

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Employee layoffs/Matthew McConaughey's salary")]

In January 2023, Salesforce reported that 8,000 employees had been laid off as a result of over-hiring during the [COVID-19 lockdowns](/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns "COVID-19 lockdowns") and a global economic downturn. In March 2023, the *[Wall Street Journal](/wiki/Wall_Street_Journal "Wall Street Journal")* reported that actor [Matthew McConaughey](/wiki/Matthew_McConaughey "Matthew McConaughey") received annual compensation of approximately $10 million as a 'creative advisor and TV pitchman.' The arrangement was disclosed amid scrutiny of executive spending during the company's major 2023 layoffs. American musician [will.i.am](/wiki/Will.i.am "Will.i.am") was also cited as being on the company's payroll due to his "strong understanding of technology".[[176]](#cite_note-176)[[177]](#cite_note-177)

### Wrongful termination lawsuit

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: Wrongful termination lawsuit")]

In September 2024, former Salesforce Senior Director Dina Zelikson filed a lawsuit against the company in [San Francisco Superior Court](/wiki/San_Francisco_County_Superior_Court "San Francisco County Superior Court"), alleging wrongful termination, discrimination, and retaliation during a medical leave.[[178]](#cite_note-178)

### 2025 layoffs and CEO declarations

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: 2025 layoffs and CEO declarations")]

In September 2025, Benioff drew criticism after announcing that Salesforce had eliminated approximately 4,000 customer service roles following the deployment of AI-powered support agents. Speaking on *The Logan Bartlett Show* podcast, he said the company reduced its support workforce from 9,000 to about 5,000 employees because he "need[ed] less heads." According to Salesforce, deployed AI agents handle approximately 50% of customer support interactions, with reported cost reductions of 17% relative to support operations in early 2025. The decision contrasted with Benioff's earlier remarks suggesting that artificial intelligence would augment, rather than replace, white-collar workers.[[179]](#cite_note-179)

### 2026 AI-focused workforce reductions

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: 2026 AI-focused workforce reductions")]

In February 2026, Salesforce initiated a new round of layoffs affecting approximately 1,000 employees across its marketing, product management, and data analytics departments.[[180]](#cite_note-180) The cuts reportedly included staff from the Agentforce AI product team, the department responsible for the company's pivot to autonomous agents.

### 2026 company kickoff and ICE controversy

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: 2026 company kickoff and ICE controversy")]

During Salesforce's annual company kickoff in February 2026, CEO Marc Benioff made a series of jokes regarding [U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement](/wiki/U.S._Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement "U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement") (ICE). During a keynote address, Benioff asked international employees to stand and then quipped that "ICE agents were in the back of the room to keep tabs on them."[[181]](#cite_note-181)

The comments caused widespread outrage on internal [Slack](/wiki/Slack_(software) "Slack (software)") channels, with employees circulating a letter demanding the company denounce ICE and halt the sale of Agentforce infrastructure to the agency. The incident led to a public rebuke from co-founder [Parker Harris](/w/index.php?title=Parker_Harris_(developer)&action=edit&redlink=1 "Parker Harris (developer) (page does not exist)"), who stated he was "not OK" with the remarks. In March 2026, company president Robin Washington held a town hall to address "internal tensions" and the resulting leaks to the press regarding the incident.[[182]](#cite_note-182)

## Salesforce Ventures

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Salesforce&action=edit&section=30 "Edit section: Salesforce Ventures")]

In 2009, Salesforce began investing in startups.[[183]](#cite_note-Peterson,_BI_8/18/2018-183) These investments became Salesforce Ventures, headed by John Somorjai.[[183]](#cite_note-Peterson,_BI_8/18/2018-183)[[184]](#cite_note-184) In September 2014, SFV set up Salesforce1 Fund, aimed at start-ups creating applications primarily for mobile phones.[[185]](#cite_note-185) In December 2018, Salesforce Ventures announced the launch of the Japan Trailblazer Fund, focused on Japanese startups.[[186]](#cite_note-186)

In August 2018, Salesforce Ventures reported investments totaling over $1 billion in 275 companies, including CloudCraze (e-commerce),[[187]](#cite_note-187) [Figure Eight](/wiki/Figure_Eight_Inc. "Figure Eight Inc.") ([artificial intelligence](/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence")),[[188]](#cite_note-188) [Forter](/wiki/Forter "Forter") (online fraud prevention),[[189]](#cite_note-189) and [FinancialForce](/wiki/FinancialForce.com "FinancialForce.com") (automation software).[[190]](#cite_note-190) In 2019, SFV's five largest investments—[Domo](/wiki/Domo_(company) "Domo (company)") (data-visualization software), [SurveyMonkey](/wiki/SurveyMonkey "SurveyMonkey") (online survey software), [Twilio](/wiki/Twilio "Twilio") (cloud-communication), [Dropbox](/wiki/Dropbox "Dropbox") (cloud storage), and [DocuSign](/wiki/DocuSign "DocuSign") (secure e-signature company)—accounted for nearly half of its portfolio.[[191]](#cite_note-Peterson_BI_11/14/2018-191) In 2021, Salesforce announced that its investments had resulted in a $2.17 billion annual gain.[[192]](#cite_note-192) In June 2023, Salesforce increased the size of its Generative AI Fund for startups from $250 million to $500 million,[[193]](#cite_note-193) and in September 2024 to $1 billion.[[194]](#cite_note-194)

To date, Salesforce Ventures has invested $5 billion in companies based in 27 different countries.[[195]](#cite_note-195)
