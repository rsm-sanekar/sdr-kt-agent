In [marketing](/wiki/Marketing "Marketing"), **lead generation** ([/ˈliːd/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English")) refers to the process of attracting and capturing consumer interest in a product or service. A lead is the contact information and, in some cases, [demographic information](/wiki/Demographic_information "Demographic information") of a customer who is interested in a specific product or service.

Leads may come from various sources or activities, for example, digitally via the [Internet](/wiki/Internet "Internet"), through personal referrals, through telephone calls either by the company or [telemarketers](/wiki/Telemarketer "Telemarketer"), through [advertisements](/wiki/Advertisement "Advertisement"), and through events.

Lead generation is often paired with [lead management](/wiki/Lead_management "Lead management") to move leads through the [purchase funnel](/wiki/Purchase_funnel "Purchase funnel"). This combination of activities is known as pipeline marketing, which is usually divided into separate marketing and sales pipelines.

## Lead scoring

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lead_generation&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Lead scoring")]

[Lead scoring](/wiki/Lead_scoring "Lead scoring") involves a [quantitative method](/wiki/Quantitative_method "Quantitative method") of assigning a numerical score to a lead. This helps the company determine whether a contact is valid for their pipeline and allows them to prioritize leads and allocate resources accordingly. The introduction of [marketing automation](/wiki/Marketing_automation "Marketing automation") has made lead scoring easier to implement.[[1]](#cite_note-1)

The score assigned to each lead is based on their level of interest, fit with the company's target market, and likelihood of becoming a paying customer. It is not static and can change based on the demographic or behavioral criteria set by the company.[[2]](#cite_note-2) Ways to audit your lead generation program include reviewing your ICP, checking data hygiene, and reviewing your tech stack integration.[[3]](#cite_note-3)

## Regulation

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lead_generation&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Regulation")]

In February 2024, the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](/wiki/Consumer_Financial_Protection_Bureau "Consumer Financial Protection Bureau") (CFPB) issued guidance targeting the manipulation of comparison-shopping tools for financial products due to kickbacks. This manipulation impacts lead generation, steering consumers towards certain products not because of their merits but due to hidden financial incentives. The guidance highlights how such practices may breach federal [consumer protection laws](/wiki/Consumer_protection_law "Consumer protection law"), emphasizing the need for unbiased, transparent comparison tools in the financial sector and offering the concept of a federal comparison shopping site as an alternative.[[4]](#cite_note-4)

The [Southwest Public Policy Institute](/wiki/Southwest_Public_Policy_Institute "Southwest Public Policy Institute")’s *Swipe Right* report[[5]](#cite_note-5) underscores the risks of excessive regulation on lead generation models. The study found that comparison-shopping tools like [Credit Karma](/wiki/Credit_Karma "Credit Karma") and [NerdWallet](/wiki/NerdWallet "NerdWallet") empower consumers with faster access to credit, especially for those excluded by traditional banks, while warning that federal overreach could diminish consumer choice and innovation.[[6]](#cite_note-6) These findings highlight the broader role of lead generation across industries: even Uber has argued in federal court that it is not a taxi company but a “lead generation” platform[[7]](#cite_note-7) connecting drivers and riders, much like [eBay](/wiki/EBay "EBay") or [Etsy](/wiki/Etsy "Etsy") connects buyers and sellers. Taken together, these examples illustrate that lead generation is not limited to marketing finance products but represents a foundational model of the digital economy, one that regulators must approach carefully to avoid undermining consumer benefits and market efficiency.
